Sole

A health companion that explains. Sole reads your steps, heart, energy, weight and blood oxygen from Apple Health, compares each one with your own normal, and answers the question you actually open the app with: how am I doing?

What the first draft got wrong

The review asked one question of every screen: does it answer "how am I doing?" in under three seconds, and can you trust the answer? Nine changes came out of it.

No single answer on TodayFour equal cards meant you still had to read and judge each one yourself.
Today opens with one headline, such as "Resting heart rate is better than usual", plus a tally: 6 usual, 1 better, 0 worth a look.
A 2×2 grid doesn't scaleThe grid broke at large text sizes and hid walking heart rate.
Vitals become a list of rows, grouped Heart, Energy, Body. Each row shows the value, a status word and its usual range in numbers.
The range bar had no numbersA dot on a band looks nice but tells you nothing exact.
Every band is labeled ("usual 58–63"). Each detail page has a How we know panel with the data behind every sentence.
Outliers poisoned "your normal"A week with flu raised your usual resting heart rate, so the next week looked fine when it wasn't.
Tag a day (sick, travel, late night, alcohol, hard workout). Tags show on charts as context, and sick or travel days are left out of your usual range.
Highlights tab was a dead endA feed you have to remember to visit, overlapping with Trends.
New highlights appear on Today and on the metric they're about. The third tab becomes Recap: a swipeable weekly story, plus past weeks.
Steps lost their "am I on track?"A percentage alone ignores that you walk more on weekdays.
The step ring gets a ghost tick for your usual pace by this time on this weekday, so you can tell at a glance whether you're ahead or behind.
No fast way to add thingsLogging weight was buried inside a card.
A floating + glass button next to the tab bar opens one sheet: log weight or tag today. The same actions are in Control Center and Siri.
First run was all permissions, no payoffThe first win came days later, once baselines filled in.
Right after Health access, Sole reads your history and shows "Here's your normal": your ranges, learned from years of data, on the first launch.
Health data on showWeight on the Lock Screen and alarming wording are real harms.
Privacy by default: Lock Screen widgets show steps only, weight can be hidden everywhere, optional Face ID lock. Wording is always neutral, with amber instead of red.

Product

Features you didn't ask for

Each one passed the same test: it makes the data easier to understand or act on, and it runs on a free developer account. The first group is recommended for v2; the second waits.

Recommended for v2

Day tags

Mark a day as sick, travel, late night, alcohol, hard workout or stressed. Spikes explain themselves on charts, and sick and travel days don't skew your normal.

Stored on device · SwiftData

Sleep as context

Last night's sleep length appears beside resting heart rate, because short sleep is the most common reason it rises. Read only; no sleep tracking screens.

Sole shows its value in the first minute by learning your ranges from your Health history straight away.

A four‑card story every Monday, with a share-as-image button. It replaces a Highlights feed nobody would remember to open.

Apple's own Highlights can't be read by any app. There is no public API for the Health app's Highlights or Trends, on any developer account. Sole rebuilds the same idea itself from your raw data, which also lets it explain things Apple's version doesn't.

Design

Liquid Glass, used with restraint

Answer, then evidence

Every screen goes headline, number, chart, then "How we know". You can stop reading at any level and still have the answer.

Glass only for controls

Glass is used for the floating tab bar, the + button, toolbar buttons, pickers, sheets and Recap controls. Content cards stay solid so numbers read clearly over the colored background.

Calm by design

Status words are Usual, Better and Worth a look. Amber, never red. Rows sort by importance, and a maximum of one notification a day.

Accessible by default

Rows reflow at any text size, VoiceOver reads each row as one sentence, charts have audio graphs, and Reduce Transparency and Reduce Motion are respected.

Living with a free account: the app has to be reinstalled from Xcode every 7 days when its signing expires. Your data stays on the phone between installs. Day tags are the one thing Health can't back up, so they're included in the CSV export.

Usual range

The middle half (25th to 75th percentile) of your daily values over the previous 28 days, leaving out days tagged sick or travel. Needs 7 days of data; until then rows say "Learning".

Usual pace

For steps, your median count by this time of day on the same weekday over the last 8 weeks. That's the tick on the ring.

Trend

Average of the last 4 weeks vs. the 12 weeks before, reported only when the change beats a minimum (for example 2 bpm for resting heart rate, 0.5 kg for weight, 10% for steps).

Headline

Picks one sentence by priority: anything worth a look first, then anything better than usual, otherwise "Everything is in your usual range". Same data, same sentence, every time.

Plan

Build order

Each step runs on your iPhone before the next starts. No v2 code has been written yet.

  1. iOS 26 and the Liquid Glass shell. Tabs (Today, Trends, Recap), the + button, gear for Settings. Steps keep working.
  2. Read the new Health types and sleep. Daily statistics, on‑device cache and history import.
  3. Your normal. Usual ranges, usual pace, statuses, trends and headline as tested Swift code.
  4. First run: "Here's your normal".
  5. Today redesign. Headline, tally, ring with pace tick, grouped vitals rows.
  6. Add sheet. Log weight to Health and tag the day.
  7. Metric detail pages. Banded charts with tag markers, How we know, sleep context.
  8. Trends tab.
  9. Highlights rules and weekly Recap. Story cards, share image, Monday notification.
  10. Widgets, Control Center and Siri. Lock Screen privacy defaults.
  11. Privacy and the doctor report. Face ID lock, hide weight, PDF export.
