Sole

From step counter to a health companion that explains. Sole reads your steps, heart, energy, weight and blood oxygen from Apple Health and tells you, in plain words, how each one compares with your own normal and which way it is heading.

Three hats

How the plan was made

Product

Every number gets a meaning

Each metric shows your usual range, its direction and one sentence on what that means. A Daily Brief leads the app; a Highlights feed replaces Apple's, which other apps can't read.

Design

Glass for controls, calm for content

Liquid Glass sits on the navigation layer only: floating tab bar, toolbar buttons, sheets. Cards stay solid so numbers are easy to read. One signature visual, the range band, appears everywhere.

Engineering

Only what a free account can ship

Every feature below uses HealthKit, Swift Charts, WidgetKit and local notifications, all available with a free personal team. Anything that needs a paid account or a hidden Apple API is left out and listed.

Apple's own Highlights can't be read by any app. There is no public API for the Health app's Highlights or Trends, on any developer account. Sole rebuilds the same idea itself from your raw data, which also lets it explain things Apple's version doesn't.

Design

Liquid Glass, used with restraint

Glass on the navigation layer

Floating tab bar that shrinks when you scroll, round toolbar buttons, segmented pickers and sheets. Content cards stay solid so numbers read clearly over any background.

A colored world behind the glass

A soft gradient sits behind every screen so the glass has something to bend. Health's own category colors (orange, red, purple, blue) keep each metric familiar.

Words before numbers

Each screen opens with a sentence, then the figure, then the chart. Status is always a word plus an arrow, never color alone.

Accessible by default

Reduce Transparency swaps glass for solid fills, Reduce Motion stops ring animation, VoiceOver reads each card as one sentence, Dynamic Type reflows cards.

Living with a free account: the app has to be reinstalled from Xcode every 7 days when its signing expires. Your data stays on the phone between installs. A free team can keep up to 3 of its own apps on a device.

Usual range

The middle half (25th to 75th percentile) of your daily values over the previous 28 days. Needs 7 days of data; until then cards say "Learning your normal".

Trend

Average of the last 4 weeks vs. the 12 weeks before, reported only when the change beats a minimum (for example 2 bpm for resting heart rate, 0.5 kg for weight, 10% for steps).

Trend weight

A smoothed average of daily weigh‑ins, so one salty dinner doesn't look like a kilo gained.

No Apple Watch?

Heart, oxygen and resting energy are usually empty without one. Those cards collapse to "Needs Apple Watch data" and the brief only talks about what exists.

Plan

Build order

Each step runs on your iPhone before the next starts. No v2 code has been written yet.

  1. iOS 26 and the Liquid Glass shell. New tabs (Today, Trends, Highlights), gear for Settings, glass toolbar. Steps keep working.
  2. Read the new Health types. Daily statistics, on‑device cache and history import; per‑metric status in Settings.
  3. Your normal. Usual ranges, statuses and trends as tested Swift code.
  4. Today redesign. Daily Brief, steps hero, vitals cards with range bands.
  5. Metric detail pages. Banded charts and explainers.
  6. Trends tab.
  7. Highlights. Rules engine with tests, then the feed.
  8. Log weight. Writes to Apple Health.
  9. Widgets and the weekly summary.
